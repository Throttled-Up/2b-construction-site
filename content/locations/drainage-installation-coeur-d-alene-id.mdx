<ServiceSection slot="section_1" heading="Why Coeur d'Alene Properties Need Proper Drainage Systems">

Coeur d'Alene sits in a convergence of conditions that punish properties without engineered drainage. From March through May, snowmelt off the surrounding mountains saturates the ground faster than most soils here can absorb it. Hillside lots above the lake — particularly in Sanders Beach, the Blackwell Island area, and the East CDA hillsides — often contain clay lenses and glacial till that trap water above gravel layers, creating perched water tables that push moisture laterally into foundations and crawl spaces.

Properties near the Spokane River outlet and the lake shoreline face seasonal groundwater rise that compounds surface runoff. Kootenai County also enforces stormwater management rules tied to Rathdrum Prairie Aquifer protection — you cannot simply redirect water off your lot without accounting for where it enters the groundwater system.

Many older cabins from the 1960s and 70s, now being torn down and replaced by high-value custom builds along the lakefront, were constructed with no engineered drainage. The new structures, often two or three times the footprint, absolutely demand it.

<ServiceSection slot="section_2" heading="Drainage Services We Provide in Coeur d'Alene">

We handle four primary drainage approaches across Coeur d'Alene properties:

**French drains** use perforated pipe bedded in gravel to intercept subsurface water on hillside lots and redirect it away from foundations. We size and grade these systems using the same soil analysis we apply to septic drain field design.

**Curtain drains** are installed uphill of a structure to catch sheet flow before it reaches the foundation. On CDA's hillside properties above the lake, this is often the first line of defense.

**Foundation perimeter drains** get installed during new construction or retrofitted on existing homes, tied to a sump or daylight outlet. We often install these as part of our [foundation excavation](/foundation-excavation-coeur-d-alene-id/) work.

**Surface grading and swales** reshape the lot to direct water away from structures, sometimes combined with subsurface systems. Learn more about our [site grading](/site-grading-coeur-d-alene-id/) and broader [excavation services](/excavating-contractor/).

<ServiceSection slot="section_5" heading="Coeur d'Alene Drainage Permits and Regulations">

Any drainage project that changes how water moves across your lot may require permits through the Kootenai County Building and Planning Department. The Rathdrum Prairie Aquifer — the sole drinking water source for roughly 500,000 people in the Spokane-Coeur d'Alene corridor — is why. Kootenai County often requires stormwater infiltration methods like dry wells or infiltration trenches rather than piping runoff to storm drains, specifically to protect aquifer recharge.

If your drainage work falls near an existing septic system or well, Panhandle Health District reviews setback distances and soil disturbance limits. We coordinate all of this before breaking ground — permit applications, health district reviews, aquifer compliance documentation — so you're not making multiple trips to county offices piecing together requirements on your own.
