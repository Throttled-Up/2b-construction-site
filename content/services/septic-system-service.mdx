## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a simple and complex septic system in Idaho?

A simple system uses gravity to move effluent from the tank to a conventional drain field. It works on relatively flat lots with good soil percolation. A complex system uses pumps, pressure distribution, mound construction, or sand filters to treat and distribute effluent on lots where gravity alone won't work: steep slopes, high water tables, poor-percolation soils, or tight setback constraints near lakes and wells. Many lakefront and hillside lots in North Idaho require complex systems. Idaho requires a separate license to install complex systems, and not all septic contractors hold it. We are licensed for both.

### How much does a new septic system cost in North Idaho?

Most residential septic systems in the region fall between $15,000 and $30,000 or more. The wide range depends on system type (gravity vs. pressure distribution vs. mound), lot difficulty (flat open land vs. steep lakefront), tank size, and drain field length. Complex systems on steep lakefront lots with tight setbacks will land at the top of or above that range. The Panhandle Health District site evaluation and perc test determine which system type your lot requires, so you don't get to choose the cheapest option if your soil or slope won't support it.

### Do I need a permit for septic work in Kootenai or Bonner County?

Yes. All septic installation, replacement, and major repair work in Kootenai and Bonner Counties requires a permit from the Panhandle Health District. The process starts with a site evaluation and soil/perc test, followed by a system design that meets Idaho DEQ standards. We handle the permit application and coordinate with the health district inspector for all required inspections during installation. Unpermitted septic work is a code violation that can result in fines and will create serious problems when you try to sell the property.

### How long does a septic system last before it needs replacement?

A well-maintained concrete septic tank can last 40-plus years. Drain fields typically last 20 to 30 years depending on soil conditions, usage patterns, and maintenance. Regular pumping extends drain field life significantly. Many systems in the Athol and North Idaho lake corridor are original 1960s and 1970s installations that are at or past end of life. Signs of a failing system include persistent slow drains, sewage odor in the yard, wet spots over the drain field, and unusually lush green patches of grass where the drain field runs.

### Why does 2B Construction use concrete septic tanks instead of plastic?

Concrete tanks are heavier, which means they resist buoyancy in the high water table conditions common near North Idaho lakes. They're structurally stronger, with no risk of deformation under soil pressure on hillside lots, and they have a proven 40-plus year lifespan. Plastic tanks are lighter and cheaper upfront but can float in saturated soil, crack under load, and have a shorter service life. For a system that costs $15,000 to $30,000 or more to install, the tank is not the place to save money.

### Can I add a septic system for an ADU or guest house on my property?

It depends on your lot. Adding an ADU or guest house may require expanding your existing septic system's capacity (a larger tank, additional drain field area) or installing a completely separate system. The Panhandle Health District will need to evaluate your lot to determine if there is adequate space and soil capacity for the additional load. Setback requirements from wells, property lines, and waterways must also be met.
